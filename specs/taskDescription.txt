The Serenity Cruises Deposit Hold Leak: Build a Payment Capture Service
The Scenario
Serenity Cruises, a luxury cruise operator based in Barcelona, has just escalated a critical issue to Yuno's technical team. They're losing approximately €280,000 per month due to a payment capture failure.

Here's what's happening: When customers book cruises (often 6-12 months in advance), Serenity takes a 20% deposit. They authorize the customer's card for the deposit amount immediately to ensure the funds are available, but they don't capture (collect) the money right away. Instead, they capture it 7 days before the cruise departure date to reduce refund overhead if customers cancel early.

The problem? Their current payment setup has no automated capture system. Their finance team manually processes captures using spreadsheets, and they're missing about 15-20% of them due to human error, timezone confusion, staff turnover, and vacation periods. When they miss the capture window, the authorization expires (most card authorizations expire after 7-30 days depending on the card network and issuer), and they have to re-authorize the customer—but by then, about 30% of those customers' cards decline (insufficient funds, expired cards, canceled cards, etc.). This represents pure revenue leakage.

Serenity processes roughly 2,800 bookings per month. They need an automated system that ensures captures happen reliably at the right time.

Domain Background: Authorization vs Capture
In the payments world, there's an important distinction between authorization and capture:

Authorization (Auth): When a merchant authorizes a payment, they're asking the customer's bank (the issuer) "Does this customer have enough funds/credit for this purchase?" If yes, the issuer places a temporary hold on those funds—the money is reserved but not yet transferred. The customer sees it as "pending" on their statement. Authorizations typically expire after 7-30 days depending on the card network (Visa, Mastercard, etc.) and issuer policies.

Capture: This is when the merchant actually collects the money. The funds move from the customer's account to the merchant's account. A capture can only happen after a successful authorization, and it must happen before the authorization expires.

Auth + Capture (or "Sale"): Most everyday purchases (like buying coffee) do both instantly—authorize and immediately capture. But in industries like hotels, car rentals, and cruises, merchants often authorize first and capture later.

This two-step flow is useful when:

You want to verify funds are available now but collect later (e.g., hotel holds)
You need to adjust the amount before capturing (e.g., restaurant tips)
You want to reduce refund processing if customers cancel before fulfillment
If a merchant fails to capture before the authorization expires, they have to start over with a new authorization—and there's no guarantee it will succeed the second time.

Your Mission
Build a payment capture scheduling service that Serenity Cruises can use to automate their delayed capture workflow. The service should allow them to schedule future captures for authorized payments and execute those captures automatically at the right time.

Functional Requirements
Core Requirements (Must Complete)
Capture Scheduling API
Build a service that allows Serenity to schedule a payment capture for a future date/time. The service should accept information about an authorized payment (you can assume it's already been authorized via Yuno's platform) and a target capture timestamp. It should store this scheduled capture and make it retrievable.

What "done" looks like: A developer at Serenity can call your API to schedule a capture for 7 days from now, and later query to see all scheduled captures (filtered by status, date range, etc.).

Capture Execution Engine
Build the backend logic that processes scheduled captures when their target time arrives. The system should detect which captures are due, attempt to execute them, handle success/failure states, and retry intelligently if a capture fails.

What "done" looks like:

Captures that are due get processed automatically (you can simulate this with a background job, cron-like scheduler, or manual trigger endpoint for demo purposes)
If a capture succeeds, its status updates to "captured" with a timestamp
If a capture fails, the system should retry (with exponential backoff or a simple retry count limit—your choice)
If a capture fails after all retries, it should be marked "failed" and the reason logged
For demo purposes, you can simulate the actual capture API call to Yuno's platform—you don't need to integrate with a real payment processor. Just simulate success/failure scenarios (e.g., 85% success rate, with random failures for insufficient funds, expired auth, network errors, etc.).
Stretch Goals (Partial Completion Expected)
Capture Deadline Alerts
Add a feature that detects captures at risk of missing their authorization expiration window. For example, if an authorization expires in 30 days but the scheduled capture is set for 35 days out, flag this as a scheduling conflict. Expose these alerts via your API or a simple dashboard/report.

Batch Capture Statistics
Provide summary analytics: total scheduled, total captured successfully, total failed, average time between scheduling and capture, success rate by date range, etc. This could be an API endpoint that returns aggregated metrics, or a simple visual report.

Test Data Requirements
To build and demo your solution, you'll need a realistic dataset. Your test data should include:

At least 100 scheduled captures across a 60-day window (past, present, and future dates)
A mix of statuses: pending, captured, failed, retrying
Booking references, customer identifiers, authorized amounts (ranging from €200 to €5,000), and scheduled capture dates
For failed captures, include realistic failure reasons: insufficient_funds, authorization_expired, card_declined, network_timeout, issuer_unavailable
At least 10-15 captures that should be processed "today" (or right now) so you can demonstrate the execution engine during your demo
You can generate this data using AI tools, spreadsheets, or code.

Acceptance Criteria
Your solution is complete when:

✅ A reviewer can schedule a new capture via your API (or UI if you build one) by providing a booking ID, authorized payment reference, amount, and target capture date/time
✅ A reviewer can retrieve all scheduled captures and filter by status or date range
✅ A reviewer can trigger (or observe) the capture execution process and see statuses update in real time or via logs
✅ Failed captures are retried automatically, and after retry exhaustion, marked as permanently failed
✅ The system handles edge cases gracefully: duplicate schedules, captures scheduled in the past, invalid data, etc.
✅ Clear documentation explains how to run the system, trigger captures, and query results
